# Why Do Humans Have Different Skin Colours? The plan

## The idea (the user picked pitch A: "the great walk")
One journey across the world and through time. We start with our shared ancestors under the strong sun of Africa and
travel with them as their descendants spread over tens of thousands of years, with a **UV gauge in the sky** that drops
as the sunlight weakens. At each stop a quick **cutaway into the skin** shows what is really happening: pigment cells
packing melanin into parcels, skin cells stacking it like umbrellas over their DNA; UV breaking folate, and UV making
vitamin D. The film ends on a world map where the sunlight map and skin colour line up, and on the point that matters:
one family, a few genes answering the sun. Length: about 4 minutes. Handled with care: science and shared ancestry
only, no ranking, no race framing; people of every skin tone drawn with the same warmth and detail.

## Look
Rich Kurzgesagt-like vector (the blood film's approach that the user said comes closer to expectations): glossy, saturated
gradients, soft bokeh, frames dense at 2-3 depths. Built from the user's reference grid (asked for in 4b) and a one-frame
look test before the build. New from the reference study (studio/research/knowledge/scoreboard.md): **cut about every
4-6 s** (7-10 cuts a minute; ours were 2-4.5), **more hues per frame** (8-9 of 12; ours 3-7), and keep shots moving.

Colour arc: hot gold savanna noon -> teal coast -> cool blue-violet north (low sun) -> white-blue Arctic with warm firelight
-> tropical greens of South Asia -> the world at dusk, warm and calm.

Pacing: TEACHING with RETENTION cuts (each beat 8-15 s, a new picture every ~5 s inside it).

## Facts (sources; web-checked 2026-11-04, hedged where uncertain)
| claim | source |
|---|---|
| Skin colour tracks UV: a compromise between protecting folate and allowing vitamin D (the vitamin D-folate theory, the most widely accepted explanation); similar skin tones evolved more than once via different variants | Jablonski & Chaplin, J. Human Evolution 2000; PBS Evolution library; Harvard "Ecologies of Skin" (Jablonski) |
| UV breaks down folate (a 1978 study: an hour of intense sun halved folate in light-skinned people); folate matters for reproduction | Branda & Eaton 1978, via Discover "The biology of skin color" |
| Loss of body hair and dark skin at least ~1.2 million years ago (dark MC1R variant dated by Rogers et al. 2004) | Rogers, Iltis & Wooding 2004 (Current Anthropology), via Jablonski's essay; PMC1288200 |
| MC1R is under strong constraint in Africa (dark pigmentation favoured) | Harding et al. 2000 (PMC1288200) |
| Main exit from Africa about 50,000-60,000 years ago (estimates range ~50-72 ka) -> "somewhere around 60,000 years ago" | phys.org 2009; Nature 2016 studies (via CS Monitor) |
| Light skin in West Eurasia under strong selection; large-effect variants (SLC24A5) brought by Neolithic farmers; dark skin common in Europe until roughly the last 8,000-10,000 years (hedged "suggests", "roughly") | Ju & Mathieson (PMC7817156; bioRxiv 2020); press summary (The Week) |
| Light skin evolved separately in Europe and East Asia through different genes | Jablonski & Chaplin (convergence); stated from knowledge (OCA2 variants in East Asia vs SLC24A5/SLC45A2 in Europe), hedged |
| Inuit skin darker than their latitude predicts; diet rich in vitamin D | Jablonski & Chaplin 2000 (stated from knowledge, widely cited) |
| African populations show wide variation in skin colour; many pigmentation variants (incl. some light ones) are old and African | Crawford et al., Science 2017 (via CARTA) |
| People have roughly similar numbers of melanocytes; differences are in the amount and type of melanin and the parcels' size | stated from knowledge (widely taught) |
| Melanin forms caps over keratinocyte nuclei (supranuclear caps) | stated from knowledge (widely taught) |

## Sound plan
Music: a warm hand-drum and kalimba theme in Africa; it travels: flute and strings on the coasts, low cold pads and wind
in the north, a throat-hum drone and crackling fire in the Arctic, sitar-like plucks in South Asia; all strands return
together in the ending. One sound per action: the gauge ticking down, footsteps on sand/snow, parcels popping into
place, the UV "zing", the vitamin D chime, folate breaking, map arrows whooshing, fire, waves, wind. Music ~70% under the voice.
