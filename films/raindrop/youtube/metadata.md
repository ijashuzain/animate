**Title:** The 10-Day Trip of a Single Raindrop
Alternatives: "Where Does Rain Come From? Follow 1 Drop" · "The Water Cycle, Told by 1 Molecule"

**Description:**
Where does rain come from? The water cycle explained by following one water molecule, Drip: how rain forms, from the ocean to a cloud to a mountain river, and back again.

Every raindrop that's ever landed on you has been an ocean, a cloud, and maybe a dinosaur's drink. Water waits thousands of years in the sea, about nine days in the air, and a couple of weeks in a river, and it has been making this trip for billions of years.

**Sources:**
- Residence times (atmosphere ~9 days, oceans ~3,000+ years, glaciers 10-1,000 years, rivers ~2 weeks): Iowa State reservoir table (Graedel & Crutzen 1993); Hawaii GG325 notes; Harvard BioNumbers 115098
- Cloud droplets form on dust, salt or smoke: USGS Water Science School, "Precipitation and the water cycle"
- Raindrop shapes (spheres and hamburger buns, not tears): USGS "Are raindrops shaped like teardrops?"; NASA GPM
- Fall speeds (cloud droplets ~1 cm/s, raindrops 4-9 m/s): UW Atmos 101
- "About a million cloud droplets per raindrop": worked out from drop sizes (~2 mm vs ~10-20 µm radius)
