# How a Volcano Builds an Island From Nothing: the plan

## The idea (picked by the user from 3 pitches, 2026-10-21): the fixed camera
A camera on a tripod is set on the dark sea floor and **never moves** while a million years pass in front of it. The
frame is a Kurzgesagt-style cross-section: the sea floor at the bottom, the sea surface across the upper third, sky above.
A clock pill in the corner speeds from "day 1" to "a million years". Everything happens inside this one frame: lava,
pillows, the mountain rising, the steam blasts, the first sunlight, life arriving, people. **The twist is that the camera
moves for the first time**: it pulls back to show the whole island chain riding the sea floor away from the hot spot, and
the next island already growing, with a second little camera waiting next to it.

## Reference look
Kurzgesagt-like, darker and more cinematic than the raindrop: deep-sea blues and teals, glowing lava oranges with soft
glows, a day/night cycle above the water that speeds up with the clock. Characters: deep-sea fish, a crab in a nightcap
asleep on a pillow lava, birds, a sea turtle, voyagers in a canoe (Cast.person), and the island itself gets a gentle
face when it first feels the sun (front face: it looks at us). Font: Nunito (labels, clock).

## Pacing
RETENTION with calm: a new event every 1-2 s inside one locked frame; 2.5 s silence before the twist. ~128 s.

## Facts (each with a source)
| claim | source |
|---|---|
| The Hawaiian islands form as the Pacific Plate moves northwest over a fixed hot spot (a mantle plume); oldest farthest away | National Geographic "Chain of islands: Hawaiian hot spot"; USGS "Hawaii hotspot" (pubs.usgs.gov/gip/hawaii); WorldAtlas |
| The sea floor there is about 5 km deep (Kamaʻehuakanaloa's base ~4,755 m below the surface) | Kamaʻehuakanaloa Seamount (Wikipedia); USGS |
| Pillow lava forms when lava erupts into water: a chilled glassy skin, inflated from inside | USGS CalVO "Don't try to have a pillow fight with these pillows"; USGS GIP Hawaii p.38 |
| Near the surface, eruptions turn explosive as water flashes to steam (shallow vents make explosion debris) | USGS "Volcanic substructure... Hawaii" (hyaloclastite from shallow vents); Surtsey 1963 (stated from knowledge) |
| Life arrives by wind, waves and wings; birds carry seeds (in guts, droppings) | Papahānaumokuākea "Wind, waves, wings"; Surtsey studies (surtsey.is; 2025 gut-dispersal study) |
| Natural arrival of new species in Hawaii: roughly one every tens of thousands of years (estimates 10,000-100,000) | Papahānaumokuākea education; BiologyInsights "How did animals get to Hawaii?" (several estimates) |
| People arrived about a thousand years ago (debated: older estimates 300-500 AD; recent dating ~1000-1200 AD) | hawaii-guide "Brief history"; recent radiocarbon studies (stated from knowledge, hedged "about") |
| The next island, Kamaʻehuakanaloa (Lōʻihi), summit ~975 m below sea level, may emerge in 10,000-100,000 years | UCSB Geography "Lōʻihi: Hawaiʻi's newest island"; Garcia et al. 2006 |
