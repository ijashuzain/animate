# How Your Eyes Turn Light Into a Picture: treatment (the redesign)

Written after the draft, with the directing method from lemo-opuscar (`DIRECTOR.md`, MIT): judge the film by **sound,
rhythm, camera, directing**, in that order. The script, the facts and the approved storyboard content stay; what changes is
how the film moves and sounds. Picture times are planned seconds (the voice arrives later and `align.py` moves everything).

## 1. Three structures, and the one we keep
1. **A single journey** (one ray from the apple to the brain, the camera never stops). Kept: the user picked it, and it
   has a natural climb back (pull out of the eye into the kitchen) that echoes the opening.
2. A before/after: the kid sees the apple; then the same second replayed in slow motion inside the eye.
3. A countdown: "0.1 s from light to picture", the clock running down beat by beat.
We keep 1, and borrow one piece of 3: a time readout that grows from 0.000 000 003 s to about 0.1 s.

## 2. Logline and arc
One red ray leaves an apple, and we ride it into a kid's eye, down to a single molecule and out to the brain, and find
that the picture we "see" is built, not received.
- **Setup** (0-32): the kitchen, the apple, the ray takes off.
- **Journey** (32-130): the eye's parts, scale shrinking, down to one molecule. The breath and the snap.
- **Turn** (130-206): a signal now, racing out; the upside-down picture is never flipped: the brain builds the scene.
- **Ending** (206-240): the climb back out to the kitchen; the kid bites the apple. Same room, new eyes.

## 3. Benchmark
- **Powers of Ten** (Charles and Ray Eames, 1977). Learn: one continuous change of scale with a calm readout riding along;
  the scale changes are the transitions. Don't take: the overhead framing, the layout of its readouts, its music.
- **Kurzgesagt explainers** (the channel's target look). Learn: rich flat vector, a glow on the hero, metaphor-free
  cutaways that stay moving. Don't take: characters (their birds), designs, colour palettes, music.

## 4. The signature shot and the transition grammar
- **Signature shot: the ride.** From the kitchen to one molecule as one move. A **scale readout** rides along in a calm
  pill (bottom-left): `1 m` (the room) -> `1 cm` (the eye) -> `1 mm` (the retina) -> `10 µm` (a cone) -> `1 nm` (the
  molecule). Each change of scale lands with a soft "turret click". At the end, **the climb**: the readout jumps back up
  (`1 mm` -> `1 cm` -> `1 m`) as the camera pulls out of the eye into the kitchen.
- **Through the light** (the medium's native move): at every change of scale the camera pushes into the red bead; it
  blooms to fill the frame; the next scale opens out of it. One grammar for the whole film, no fades.
- **Turn the model**: when the view changes from the cutaway to the face-on iris (and back), the cutaway turns: it
  squashes to an edge and opens as the iris.
- Inside one scene, cuts are match cuts on motion (the ray keeps its screen direction).

Sound as transition: **L-cut** at 22 (the kitchen birds run over the cut into the ride), **J-cut** at 129.2 (the signal
arpeggio starts before the cut at 130), **J-cut** at 225 (the kitchen motif returns a second before the climb lands in the kitchen at 226).
Silences: 124.3-125.5 (before the snap), 190.8-192.2 (before the turn).

## 8. Words on screen
Baloo 2 in calm pills beside the action (the house rule). Numbers, names and scale labels only. Every pill holds at least
letters / 15 + 1.5 s (checked with `node tools2/film.mjs eyes readcheck`).

## 9. Checks before delivery (from lemo / raea, now in CLAUDE.md)
Model sheet of the kid (stills/model-sheet.png) and an action strip of the bite at 0.2 s; readcheck; two silences and
two J/L-cuts in the mix; subject at least a third of the frame height at key moments; final mix at about -14 LUFS
(`final` measures and normalises it); when the voice arrives, a speech-to-text check of every line.
