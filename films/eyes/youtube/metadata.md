**Title:** How Your Eyes Turn Light Into a Picture
Alternatives: "You Don't See With Your Eyes" · "Ride One Ray of Light Into Your Brain"

**Description:**
How do your eyes see? We follow one ray of red light from an apple into a kid's eye and all the way to the brain: through the cornea, the pupil and the lens, across the jelly that fills the eyeball, through the backwards-built retina to a single red-sensitive cone, and into one molecule that snaps straight in less than a trillionth of a second. That snap is the very first step of seeing.

Then the light becomes a signal. Nerve cells in the retina pass it on through the optic nerve, a cable of about a million fibres (past the blind spot you never notice), across the crossing where left and right swap sides, to the visual cortex at the back of your head, in less than a tenth of a second. And the upside-down picture on your retina? Your brain never flips it. It reads patterns of signals and builds the scene the right way up. You don't really see with your eyes: you see with your brain.

**Sources:**
- The cornea provides about two thirds of the eye's focusing power; the lens adjusts focus for distance (accommodation): Marist College human vision notes; UMass open textbook
- Light passes through the retina's neural layers before reaching the photoreceptors: McGill University, "The Brain from Top to Bottom", anatomy of the retina
- About 120 million rods and about 6 million cones (6-7 million): Marist College; most physiology textbooks
- Retinal's 11-cis to all-trans isomerization takes about 200 femtoseconds (Schoenlein et al., Science 1991), about 30 fs in a 2015 study (Max Planck / phys.org): "less than a trillionth of a second"
- At the optic chiasm the nasal fibres cross, so each hemisphere receives the opposite half of the visual field: lens.com "The visual pathway"; ARH Foundation
- Signals reach the primary visual cortex within tens of milliseconds (about 40-100 ms): eNeuro 2020 (C1 latency): "less than a tenth of a second"
- Stated from knowledge (hedged): sunlight contains all visible colours and red objects reflect mostly red; light crosses a room in a few billionths of a second; the pupil narrows in bright light and widens in the dark; the retinal image is inverted; three cone types; the optic nerve carries about a million fibres; the blind spot has no photoreceptors and the brain fills it in; the cortex handles edges, colour, form and motion in partly separate areas and combines them
