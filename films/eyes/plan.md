# How Your Eyes Turn Light Into a Picture: the plan

## The idea (the user picked pitch A: follow one ray of light)
One continuous journey with a camera that never stops: sunlight hits a red apple, and we ride one ray of red light off the
apple, across the room, into an eye: through the cornea, the pupil and the lens, across the jelly, through the backwards
retina to one red-sensitive cone, into one molecule (retinal) that snaps straight, then out as a signal through the optic
nerve, across the chiasm, to the visual cortex at the back of the head. The twist: the image on the retina is upside down,
but the brain never "flips a picture"; it reads signals and builds the scene. Ending: you see with your brain.
Length: about 4 minutes.

## Look
Rich Kurzgesagt-like vector, the blood/skin films' recipe (the user's reference grid, then a one-frame look test). The eye's
interior glows: deep blue-violet jelly, pink-orange retina, cones and rods as glossy columns, nerve signals as bright
travelling sparks. Pacing from the reference study: a new shot every ~5 s planned (3-4 s after the voice); here many cuts
are "push-throughs" along the ray, so the journey reads as one move. Hero: the red ray (a glowing red bead with a trail).
Colour arc: warm daylight kitchen -> cool blue eye interior -> pink retina -> violet-gold molecule -> electric cyan signals
-> deep blue brain with golden pathways -> back to the warm kitchen.

| 130-142 | That tiny change sets off a chain reaction, and the cone fires an electrical signal. | a cascade of molecules lighting up; a spark leaves the cone |
| 142-154 | Nerve cells in the retina collect the signals and send them out through the optic nerve, a cable of about a million fibres. | sparks hop from cell to cell; the fibres bundle into the optic nerve |

| 178-192 | Then they race to the back of your head, to the visual cortex, in less than a tenth of a second. | the signals travel to the back of the brain; a millisecond clock; the visual cortex glows |
| 192-206 | And the upside-down picture? Your brain never sees a picture at all. It reads patterns of signals, and builds the scene the right way up. | the upside-down image dissolves into signals; the cortex rebuilds the apple, upright |
| 206-220 | Edges, colours, shapes and movement are worked out separately, then put back together into one view. | areas light up: edges, colour, shape, motion; their pieces merge into the apple |
| 220-232 | And all of that happens again and again, for millions of rays, every moment your eyes are open. | pull back: rays pouring into the eye from the whole kitchen |
| 232-240 | So you don't really see with your eyes. You see with your brain. Your eyes are just where the light gets in. | the kid bites the apple and smiles; end |

## Facts (sources; web-checked 2026-11-05, hedged where uncertain)
| claim | source |
|---|---|
| Sunlight contains all visible colours; red objects reflect mostly red and absorb the rest | stated from knowledge (basic optics) |
| Light crosses a few metres in a few billionths of a second (3x10^8 m/s) | stated from knowledge |
| The cornea provides about two-thirds of the eye's focusing power; the lens adjusts for distance (accommodation) | Marist human-vision notes; UMass open book |
| The pupil is set by iris muscles (constrict in bright light, dilate in dim light) | stated from knowledge |
| The retinal image is inverted | stated from knowledge (basic optics) |
| Light passes through the retina's neural layers before reaching the photoreceptors | McGill "The brain from top to bottom", anatomy of the retina |
| About 120 million rods and about 6 million cones (6-7 million) | Marist; Physics of Eyes course; most textbooks |
| Three cone types (long, medium, short wavelength: reddish, greenish, bluish) | stated from knowledge |
| Retinal's 11-cis to all-trans isomerization: ~200 fs (Schoenlein et al., Science 1991); ~30 fs in a 2015 study -> "less than a trillionth of a second" | Schoenlein 1991; Max Planck / phys.org 2015 |
| The optic nerve carries about a million fibres (about 1.2 million) | stated from knowledge (hedged "about") |
| The optic disc has no photoreceptors (the blind spot); the brain fills it in | stated from knowledge (widely taught) |
| At the optic chiasm nasal fibres cross, so each hemisphere receives the opposite visual field | lens.com "the visual pathway"; ARH Foundation |
| Signals reach the primary visual cortex within tens of milliseconds (~40-100 ms) -> "less than a tenth of a second" | eNeuro 2020 (C1 latency); lecture notes |
| The cortex processes edges, colour, form and motion in partly separate areas and combines them | stated from knowledge (hedged) |

## Sound plan
Music: a bright, curious plucked theme in the kitchen; it speeds up and thins into a single glassy tone as we ride the ray;
inside the eye, wide airy pads; in the retina, a soft rhythmic pulse; at the molecule, a held breath then a crisp chime;
electric arpeggios for the signals; a warm full theme when the brain rebuilds the apple; back to the kitchen theme.
One sound per action: whoosh of the ray, a "tink" at the cornea, iris muscles whirring, lens stretching, the molecule's
snap, signal blips, the chiasm crossing, a soft crunch of the apple. Music ~9 dB under the voice.
