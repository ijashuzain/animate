# How a Spider Builds Its Web in One Night: treatment

Lemo-opuscar's directing method (sound, rhythm, camera, directing) in its Watercolor Brush grammar. Planned seconds;
the voice arrives later and `align.py` moves everything.

## 1. Three structures, and the one we keep
1. **One night, one page** (kept: the user's pick). A locked journal sheet; the web paints itself in real order while a
   hand-written clock note ticks from dusk to dawn. The page is the stage, the night is the clock.
2. Ride the first thread: a lateral dolly across a painted garden following the bridge line, then page turns per stage.
3. Backwards from dawn: the finished dewy web un-paints to its first thread, then is built forwards with labels.
We borrow one piece of 3 for the ending: at dawn the web mists back to paper thread by thread as the spider eats it.

## 2. Logline and arc
Between two twigs at dusk there is nothing; by dawn there is a trap of thirty spokes and a glue spiral, built in about an
hour by one spider, and by morning it is eaten and gone.
- **Setup** (0-32): an empty page; the problem (a gap too wide to jump); the first thread on the breeze; the bridge.
- **Build** (32-160): the silk itself; Y and hub; frame; spokes; scaffolding; the sticky spiral; why it doesn't stick.
- **Turn** (160-186): the spider waits; silence; a moth hits; the web rings and holds.
- **Ending** (186-220): dawn and dew; the web is eaten; two twigs on an empty page again (bookend).

## 3. Benchmark
- **Naturalist field journals and botanical plates** (specimen + hand note). Learn: one subject per view, pen leader lines,
  generous paper margins, colour as information. Don't take: any specific artwork, layout or lettering.
- **Time-lapse nature cinematography of orb weavers** (the real build order and rhythm). Learn: the build accelerates
  (slow bridge, quick spokes, long patient spiral), the stillness of waiting. Don't take: shots or framings.

## 4. The signature shot and the transition grammar
- **Signature shot: the whole web paints itself on one locked page** (radii plucked out one by one, each a rising note,
  then the spiral winding inward), the clock note ticking in the corner. The film returns to this wide page between
  every detail, so the web visibly grows across the film.
- **Transition grammar from the medium**: details open as **specimen insets painted onto the same sheet** (a wash circle
  blooms beside the web, the detail paints in, a pen leader joins it to its place), and close with **mist** (the inset
  washes back to paper). Time passing = the **sky wash changing** on the locked page (dusk violet -> night blue -> dawn gold).
  No cuts between unrelated pictures; the page never leaves.

## 8. Words on the page
Hand notes in Caveat (labels with leader lines), measured labels in a small mono, the title in Cormorant Garamond italic.
Words only for names, numbers and the clock. Every note holds at least letters / 15 + 1.5 s (readcheck).

## 9. Characters and model sheet
The spider (a garden orb weaver) and the moth: built from the user's reference images (requested), painted in the brush
style (washes for bodies, bristle strokes for legs, no outlines). Model sheet (film.mjs model): the spider top and side
views, 3 key poses (lifting the abdomen to release silk, walking a thread, waiting head-down at the hub), moods through
pose only (no face), the palette.
