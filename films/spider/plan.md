# How a Spider Builds Its Web in One Night: the plan

## The idea (the user picked pitch A: one night, one page)
One sheet of a naturalist's journal, two twigs painted on it, the camera locked like someone watching quietly from dusk to
dawn. The web paints itself thread by thread in the real order (bridge line, Y and hub, frame, spokes, temporary spiral,
sticky spiral), a garden spider doing the work. A hand-written clock note in the corner ticks the hours. Specimen insets
(spinnerets, a leg, one thread magnified) paint in beside the web with pen leader lines. Ending: the spider eats its web at
dawn; the page mists back to two twigs. Length: about 3:40.

## Look (lemo-opuscar "Watercolor Brush", grammar only)
A hand-painted journal page that paints itself: warm cotton paper with fibres; everything made of translucent brush strokes
with dry-brush flying white (bristle tracks that break into dashes); washes in three jittered passes so edges pool; no
vector outlines. Ink (warm near-black, a lighter brown) only for silk, stems, notes and leader lines. Hand notes in a
handwriting face (Caveat), measured labels in a small mono, the title in a serif (Cormorant Garamond). Paper is the white.
Palette (3-5 hues + ink): dusk violet-grey sky wash `#b9aec8`, twig umber `#7a5a3c`, leaf sage `#8ea860` / shade `#4e6e3c`,
spider ochre `#c08a3e` with brown `#5a3a22`; one accent, vermilion `#c8442a`, for the hub and the clock note.
Colour arc: a dusk wash (violet-grey) -> deep blue night (a moon wash) -> pale gold dawn; the paper itself warms with time.
Pacing: TEACHING with motion: something is always being painted (a thread grows, a note writes itself); a new beat every
~8-12 s planned; the camera moves inside the sheet (slow pushes into details, a pull back to the whole page).

## Sound plan (TREATMENT.md has the cue map)
Music: acoustic and gentle, on real samples (tools2/lemo): fingerpicked nylon guitar and harp at dusk, kalimba and
glockenspiel for the threads, soft flute and bowed vibraphone at night, a warmer string trio at dawn. One sound per action:
a paper brush rustle for every painting-in, a pencil scratch for every note, a breeze for the drifting thread, a soft
"tink" as the bridge snags, a pluck for every spoke (rising pitch: the web as an instrument), glue-drop plinks, crickets at
night, the moth's flutter and the web's ring, birds at dawn. Silence (the brush lifted) before the moth hits.
