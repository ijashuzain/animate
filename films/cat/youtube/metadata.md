**Description:**
Why do cats always land on their feet? A cat slips off a bookcase and turns right-side up in about a third of a second. We slow the fall down millisecond by millisecond and look inside the cat as it happens: the balance organ in its inner ear, the signal racing to its legs, and the bendy spine that lets its front and back halves turn one after the other.

It starts in the ear: tiny crystals in a fluid-filled sac slide toward the ground and tell the brain which way is down, while the eyes look for the floor. Then the cat bends in the middle. With its front legs tucked, the front half spins round fast while the stretched-out back half barely moves; then they swap, and the back half follows less than a tenth of a second later. Legs out, back arched, soft paws: landed. Kittens start learning this at about three weeks old, and it isn't magic: from a short fall there may not be time to finish the turn.
