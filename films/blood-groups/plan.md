# Why Do We Have Different Blood Types? The plan

## The idea (the user picked pitch B: one red blood cell's life)
We follow one real red blood cell from its birth in the bone marrow to the end of its 120 days. Along the way it gets its
blood-group "tag" (real sugar chains added by a real enzyme), rides the bloodstream, meets the antibodies that guard the
blood, and sees what happens when the wrong blood arrives in a transfusion. Then the camera zooms all the way out from the
cell to the family tree of primates: the same A/B gene is at least 20 million years old, and germs are the best answer to
why the variety survives. Length: up to 4 minutes (the user allowed it).

## Look
Flat vector in the Kurzgesagt sense: rich reds and warm pinks inside the body, deep plum shadows, glowing plasma; a clean
microscope world with real shapes (biconcave red cells, Y-shaped antibodies, sugar chains as beads on stalks). Real scale
pills at each zoom (mm -> um -> nm). Our hero cell is marked by a soft glow so we can follow it in a crowd. **Before the
build: reference images from the user and a one-frame look test** (lesson from the canyon film).

Colour arc: marrow (warm cream and coral) -> bloodstream (deep red, plum) -> the attack (red into dark, flashes of white)
-> the ancestors (golden sunlit forest, savanna) -> germs (sickly greens and purples) -> back to calm red.

Pacing: TEACHING (one idea at a time, 6-15 s per beat), with a new picture at least every 2-3 s inside each beat.

## Facts (sources; web-checked 2026-10-30, hedged where science is unsure)
| claim | source |
|---|---|
| ABO A/B variation is a trans-species polymorphism shared by descent with gibbons, at least ~20 million years old | Ségurel et al., PNAS 2012 (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3494955); Science News |
| The ABO gene (chromosome 9) makes a glycosyltransferase: A adds N-acetylgalactosamine, B adds galactose, to the H antigen; A and B enzymes differ by 4 amino acids | Wikipedia "ABO (gene)"; OMIM 110300 |
| The O allele: a single-base deletion -> frameshift -> inactive, truncated enzyme; O cells keep the bare H chain | OMIM 110300; Wikipedia "ABO (gene)" |
| Anti-A/anti-B are absent at birth, appear at ~3-6 months, probably triggered by gut bacteria carrying similar sugars | review PMC7449879; U. Alberta transfusion module (hedged: "probably") |
| Bone marrow makes about 2 million red cells per second; they live about 100-120 days | Macmillan Life 11e; textbook calculation (5 L x 5 million/mm3) |
| Red cells are about 7-8 um wide, lose their nucleus, biconcave | stated from knowledge (widely taught) |
| Blood circulates round the body in about a minute | stated from knowledge (hedged "about") |
| Mismatched transfusion: antibodies bind, cells clump (agglutinate) and burst (haemolysis) | stated from knowledge (widely taught) |
| Group O linked to less severe malaria (66% lower odds in Mali); rosetting prefers A > B > O cells; evidence observational | Rowe et al. 2007 (PMC2077280); 2023 genotype study (PMC10522014); systematic review (mixed results) -> hedged "seems" |
| Group O linked to more severe cholera (not more infection) | review PMC4973168 |
| O-negative red cells can be given to almost anyone in emergencies | stated from knowledge (hedged "almost") |
| Landsteiner discovered ABO in 1901 (not in the script; for the description) | stated from knowledge |
