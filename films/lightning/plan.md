# How a Cloud Builds a Lightning Bolt: the plan

## The idea (direct, the user's rule: no metaphors)
A real thundercloud in cross-section, then one strike in extreme slow motion. Hook: a flash over a field, the bang 9 s later;
"that bolt took a fifth of a second. Inside it, a lot happened." The cloud: warm humid air rises (updraft arrows), water
freezes higher up, hail (graupel) and ice crystals collide; small crystals ride up positive, heavy graupel falls negative;
the cloud splits into charged layers. The strike, slowed 1,000x with a timer: the stepped leader zigzags down in steps, a
streamer reaches up from a tree, they meet, the return stroke flashes UP, dart leaders re-strike (flicker), the air
expands: thunder. Small living characters in the real scene: a farmer and a dog heading inside, cows, birds, a family
counting seconds at a window.

## Look
Flat vector, dramatic stormy blues and purples; the bolt is electric white with a blue-violet glow; charges as small +/-
dots (real charge, drawn simply). Font: Lilita One for numbers.

## Script (scratch timing)
| t | line | on screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0-10 | Flash. One, two, three... nine seconds, then the bang. That bolt took about a fifth of a second. Here's how a cloud builds it. | a field at dusk; a flash; a family at a window counts; thunder; the bolt freezes in place |
| 10-24 | It starts with warm, wet air rising fast, sometimes faster than a car on a highway, up to ten kilometres high. | updraft arrows lift moist air; the cloud towers up into an anvil; a height ruler to 10 km |
| 24-36 | Up there it's far below freezing. Water turns into tiny ice crystals and soft hail called graupel. | cutaway: droplets freeze into crystals and graupel pellets |
| 36-50 | They crash into each other millions of times. Each bump moves a little electric charge: small crystals become positive, heavier graupel negative. | close-up collisions; + and - signs appear on the particles |
| 50-62 | The updraft carries the light crystals to the top, and the heavy graupel sinks. The cloud splits: positive above, negative below. | the charges sort into layers; the ground below turns positive under the cloud's base |
| 62-72 | When the difference gets huge, the air can't hold it anymore. Let's slow the strike down a thousand times. | a timer at microseconds; the cloud base crackles |
| 72-84 | An invisible channel, the stepped leader, zigzags down in jumps of about fifty metres, branching as it looks for the ground. | the leader steps down, branching, faint violet |
| 84-94 | When it gets close, the ground reaches back. A spark called a streamer rises from the tallest thing nearby: this tree. | a streamer climbs from the treetop; others from a fence post and the barn's lightning rod |
| 94-96.5 | (silence) | the leader and the streamer a hand's width apart, frozen |
| 96.5-108 | They touch, and a huge current races back up the channel at a third of the speed of light. That's the flash you see. It goes up, not down. | the return stroke blazes upward; the whole channel lights white; label "about 30,000 amperes" |
| 108-118 | More leaders often follow the same path, which is why lightning flickers. And the air in the channel heats so fast it explodes outward: thunder. | 2-3 re-strikes flicker; a shock ring expands; the timer back to normal speed |
| 118-128 | Sound travels about a kilometre every three seconds. Nine seconds: three kilometres away. Time to stay inside. | sound ring travels to the house; the family counts 9; "about 3 km" pill; rain begins |

## Facts (sources)
| claim | source |
|---|---|

| stepped leader meets an upward streamer; the return stroke propagates upward; flash looks like it descends | NWS "Lightning science: return stroke" |

| stepped leader to return stroke ~1/133 s; dart leaders give the flicker | NWS slow-motion flashes |
| leader steps of about 50 m; thunder from rapidly heated, expanding air; ~3 s per km | stated from knowledge (hedged "about") |
