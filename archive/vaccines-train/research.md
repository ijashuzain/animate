# How do vaccines train your body? — research

**The one true thing:** a vaccine shows the immune system a germ's "face" (its antigens) without the danger, so the
matching cells multiply and leave memory cells behind; the next meeting is fast.

## Facts (with sources)
- Germs carry antigens; the immune system recognises them (textbook immunology).
- First (primary) response: antibody levels rise over about 1–2 weeks (British Society for Immunology resource);
  memory B and T cells persist after vaccination (Nature 2021 mRNA study: memory B cells in all participants at 6 months;
  for many vaccines memory lasts years to decades — stated with "can").
- Vaccine types: weakened (live attenuated), inactivated (killed), subunit/protein (a piece), mRNA (instructions).
- Dendritic ("sentinel") cells carry antigen to lymph nodes and present it; matching B cells make antibodies (Y-shaped);
  killer (cytotoxic) T cells destroy infected cells.
- Smallpox: Jenner's 1796 vaccine was the first successful vaccine; WHO declared smallpox eradicated in 1980, the only
  human infectious disease eradicated. — WHO, Smallpox.
- Immunisation saved at least 154 million lives over 50 years (Lancet study, announced by WHO, April 2024).
