# How do bees make honey? — research

**The one true thing:** honey is nectar that bees have processed with enzymes and dried by fanning until it is about
17–18% water, then sealed with wax; one worker makes only about 1/12 teaspoon in her life.

## Facts (with sources)
- Foragers store nectar in the honey stomach (crop); in the hive it is passed mouth to mouth to house bees, who add
  invertase (sucrose → glucose + fructose). — NC State University News, "How do bees make honey?" (2013); Bee Culture.
- Nectar is roughly 70–80% water; fanning drives it down to about 17–18%; cells are then capped with beeswax made in
  glands on the bees' abdomens. — NC State; Blue Sky Science (Morgridge Institute).
- Summer workers live about five to six weeks (University of Tennessee Extension); Agroscope gives 3–6 weeks.
- One worker makes about 1/12 teaspoon of honey in her life; about 2 million flowers and ~55,000 miles of flying per
  pound (University of Illinois Extension; widely quoted National Honey Board figures).
- Honey is the colony's winter food; strong colonies store a surplus that beekeepers harvest (general beekeeping knowledge).

## Hedges
- "around seventy to eighty percent", "about seventeen or eighteen percent", "about five or six weeks", "about a twelfth".
- "Hundreds of bees" per jar: estimates of 550–770 bee-lifetimes per pound vary; said as "hundreds".
