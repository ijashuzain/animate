## Plain lines (paste into ElevenLabs, one file each)

1. Nineteen sixty-nine. Two computers, hundreds of miles apart, try to talk to each other for the very first time. The message was meant to be "login". The system crashed after just two letters: L, O.
2. Nineteen seventy-one. Ray Tomlinson sends the first email between computers on a network, and picks the at sign to join a person's name to the name of their machine.
3. Nineteen eighty-three. The network switches to a shared language called TCP/IP. Every message is cut into small packets, each packet finds its own way across the network, and they're put back together, in order, at the other end.
4. Nineteen ninety-one. At CERN, the physics lab in Switzerland, Tim Berners-Lee puts the very first website online. Pages that link to other pages, anywhere in the world: he calls it the World Wide Web.
5. Nineteen ninety-eight. Google ranks pages by how many other pages link to them, like votes. The most linked page rises to the top, and finding almost anything becomes easy.
6. Two thousand four. Social networks arrive. First they connect friends and classmates, then strangers by the billion. Suddenly anyone can share a photo, a thought or a video with the whole world.
7. Two thousand seven. The iPhone puts the whole internet in your pocket. Maps, music, photos and messages: now the internet goes everywhere you go, every hour of the day.
8. Today, about five and a half billion people are online. That's two out of every three humans on Earth, all connected by the same simple idea: small packets of data, finding their own way from one computer to another.
9. And it all started with two letters, typed on a quiet night in nineteen sixty-nine. L, O. As in: lo, and behold. Not a bad first word for the internet.
