## Plain lines (paste into ElevenLabs, one file each)

1. Earth is spinning you east at more than one thousand kilometres an hour. If it stopped suddenly, you'd be flung sideways and die in seconds. What would really happen?
2. One second later, everything not bolted to the bedrock keeps moving east at full speed. Air, oceans, rocks, cities, and you, all flung sideways faster than a jet airliner. Only the poles stay calm.
3. A minute later, winds far stronger than any hurricane scour the land, and the oceans keep surging east as giant tsunamis, crashing over the continents. That's the sudden version. Now let's be kinder.
4. Imagine the spin fades slowly instead, over many years. Now one day lasts a whole year: about six months of daylight, then six months of night, as the Sun slowly crawls across the sky.
5. Spinning makes Earth bulge at its middle: the equator sits about twenty-one kilometres further out than the poles, and the oceans pile up there. Without the spin, that water slowly flows toward the poles.
6. One computer model shows where it ends up: two huge polar oceans, and one giant continent wrapped around the middle of the planet. Greenland and Canada would be under water.
7. And there's a quieter danger. Earth's magnetic field is made by molten iron swirling inside, and that swirling depends partly on the spin. Some scientists think the field could fade, leaving us exposed to the Sun's radiation.
8. The good news? Earth is slowing down, but only by about two thousandths of a second every century. Four hundred million years ago, a day lasted about twenty-two hours. At that pace, you have nothing to worry about.
9. So tonight, as the Sun sets right on time, remember: you're riding a planet whose middle spins at more than sixteen hundred kilometres an hour. And that's exactly how we like it.
