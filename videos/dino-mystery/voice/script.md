1. Nobody saw the dinosaurs die. But sixty-six million years later, you can still touch the clues the killer left. They're hidden in a thin layer of clay.
2. The victims: about three quarters of all plant and animal species, including T. rex and Triceratops. The suspects: disease, a changing climate, giant volcanoes, or something from space. Let's look at the evidence.
3. Clue one: the clay. All over the world, rocks from that moment share one thin layer. Below it, dinosaur fossils. Above it, none, ever again. Whatever happened, it happened fast, in geological terms.
4. Clue two: in nineteen eighty, Luis and Walter Alvarez found that this clay is loaded with iridium, hundreds of times more than normal. Iridium is rare on Earth's surface, but common in asteroids.
5. Clue three: inside the layer, there's shocked quartz, crystals cracked by a sudden, enormous pressure, and tiny glass beads, melted rock that rained back down from the sky. Both are the fingerprints of an impact.
6. Clue four: the weapon. Buried under Mexico's Yucatán coast is a crater about one hundred and eighty kilometres wide, called Chicxulub. It's the same age as the clay. A rock around ten kilometres across hit right here.
7. But there's a second suspect. Around the same time, enormous volcanic eruptions in India, the Deccan Traps, poured out lava and gases over a very long time. Scientists still debate how much they helped.
8. And here's the twist: not every dinosaur died. Birds are dinosaurs, and scientists think a few small, ground-dwelling birds made it through the darkness that followed. Every pigeon, chicken and sparrow today descends from those survivors.
9. So, case closed: an asteroid, maybe with help from volcanoes. The proof was in the clay. And the next time a bird lands outside your window, say hello to a dinosaur.
