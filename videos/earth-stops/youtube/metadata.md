## Description

What happens if Earth stops rotating? Second by second: the winds, the oceans, a day that lasts a year, and why Earth slowing down is nothing to worry about.

Earth's equator spins east at about 1,670 km/h. If it stopped suddenly, everything would keep moving: winds and tsunamis would scour the planet. If it slowed gently, a day would last a year, the oceans would drain toward the poles and leave one giant continent around the middle, and the magnetic field might fade. And the good news: Earth really is slowing, but only by about 2 milliseconds per century.

📚 Sources
- Space.com, "What would happen if Earth stopped spinning?" (physicist Andrew Layden): everything keeps moving; poles least affected; a gradual stop gives ~6 months of day and ~6 of night
- ScienceAlert: the equator moves east at about 1,674 km/h
- Popular Science / Esri (W. Fraczek, 2010): a non-spinning Earth model, oceans migrate to the poles, one equatorial megacontinent (IFLScience: Greenland and Canada submerged; magnetic field could be lost)
- Earth's day lengthens about 2 ms per century (tidal drag); ~400 million years ago a day was about 22 hours (coral growth rings, ~400 days a year)
