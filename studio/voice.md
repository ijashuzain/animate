# The channel's voice

Read before writing any voice line, title or description (idea from youtube-agent-skill's voice.md: a script in the
wrong voice is worse than none, because someone has to read it out loud). The narrator is the user, via ElevenLabs.

**Who it's for:** curious people of any age, 10 to 70, watching on a phone. No prior knowledge assumed.

**How it sounds:**
- Calm, warm, a little playful; talks *to* the viewer ("you", "your body", "your phone") in the first six words.
- Short spoken sentences; one idea per sentence; about 2.7 words a second.
- Concrete over grand: a number, a name, a date or a thing you can picture beats an adjective.
- Numbers are written as words in voice lines (the narrator reads them); digits are fine on screen and in titles.
- Hedges where science is uncertain: "about", "could", "scientists think". Never invents a figure or a source.

**Never says:** amazing, incredible, insane, mind-blowing, game-changer, secret, ultimate, "in this video",
"welcome back", "before we start", "don't forget to subscribe", "smash that like button".

**Shape of every script** (from the hook research):
1. The first sentence confirms what the title promised and opens a question the viewer can't close yet.
2. By 0:15 the viewer knows the payoff exists. No channel intro, ever.
3. One idea per scene; every scene shows something on screen that matches the line.
4. The payoff is said plainly when it lands ("that's why...").
5. One ending line, a callback to the opening. At most one ask, and only if it's earned.
