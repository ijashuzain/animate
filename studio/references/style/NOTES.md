# The channel's style library (references from the user, 2026-10-22)
Use these for ANY new animal or nature prop, in every film, before inventing a look.
- animals-flat-1/2/3: big sheets of flat cartoon animals. What they share (the house style for creatures):
  - no outlines; 2-3 flat tones per animal (a body colour, a darker shade for the far legs/under side, a light belly
    or muzzle); simple rounded silhouettes; thin legs; small heads relative to the body for big animals, big heads for
    small ones;
  - side view (profile) for most, one small black eye with a tiny white highlight, a short curved smile; the head is
    sometimes turned 3/4 toward us;
  - soft natural palette (browns, ochres, dusty pinks, blue-greys, olive greens), never neon;
  - small details only where they identify the animal: spots, stripes, a shell pattern, antlers, a mane.
  - Useful for the canyon: the orange lizard and the green chameleon (sheet 2, bottom row), the crab (sheet 2), the
    snake/rattlesnake (sheet 3), the eagle/condor-like birds (sheet 3). No trilobite here: draw it in this style
    (segmented oval shell, three lobes, small antennae, a tiny profile eye).
- animals-stylised-4: a more graphic, editorial variant (bold geometric shapes, muted pink/teal/orange, hatching lines on
  fur): use only when a film wants a more "designed" look; it has fine line texture, so keep it off text backgrounds.
- nature-faceted: low-poly faceted nature (trees, mountains, stump, clouds, flowers) with a white sticker edge. The
  facet idea matches the canyon references (angular lit/shade planes on rock): use facets for rocks and mountains; skip
  the sticker edge unless a film wants a cut-out look.
