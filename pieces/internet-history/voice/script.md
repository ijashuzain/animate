## Plain lines

1. Nineteen sixty-nine. Two computers, hundreds of miles apart, try to talk for the first time. The message was meant to be "login". The system crashed after two letters: L, O.
2. Nineteen seventy-one. Ray Tomlinson sends the first network email, and picks the at sign to join a person's name to their computer.
3. Nineteen eighty-three. The network switches to TCP/IP: messages are cut into small packets, each finds its own way, and they're put back together at the other end.
4. Nineteen ninety-one. At CERN, Tim Berners-Lee puts the first website online. Pages that link to other pages: the World Wide Web.
5. Nineteen ninety-eight. Google ranks pages by how many other pages link to them, and finding anything becomes easy.
6. Two thousand four. Social networks connect friends, then strangers by the billion. Everyone becomes a publisher.
7. Two thousand seven. The iPhone puts the internet in your pocket. Now it goes everywhere you go.
8. Today, about five and a half billion people are online. Two out of every three humans on Earth.
9. And it all started with two letters. L, O. As in: lo, and behold.
